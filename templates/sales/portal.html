💡 Sales Tips for the Ghanaian Market
01
Lead with the Loan — Price is Not the Barrier
Most Ghanaians can't buy outright. Don't open with the full price — open with the monthly payment. "You can drive this car for less than GH₵ X per month through Republic Bank." Financing unlocks buyers who would otherwise walk away.
02
WhatsApp is Your Showroom
Ghana runs on WhatsApp. Post vehicle photos, videos and prices in relevant groups every day. Use your referral link in every message. Status updates reach your entire contact list — use them consistently. Short video walkthroughs convert better than still photos.
03
Target Employers, Not Just Individuals
A single HR or procurement officer at a bank, NGO or mining company can place an order for 5–20 vehicles at once. Use the proposal PPTX to pitch to companies. One corporate deal is worth more than 20 individual sales. Go where the money is managed.
04
Relationships First — Trust Before Transaction
In Ghana, people buy from people they trust. Visit prospects in person when you can. Greet them properly, ask about their family, show genuine interest. Follow up with a WhatsApp message the same day. A relationship built over weeks converts better than a cold pitch.
05
Sell the Cost-Saving Story for Solar
ECG outages cost Ghanaian businesses thousands per month in generator fuel, downtime and spoilage. Show the solar system as an investment, not an expense: "You spend GH₵ X on fuel every month — this system pays itself off in Y months and then it's free power." That framing closes deals.
06
Churches & Religious Organisations are Major Buyers
Large churches in Ghana have significant purchasing power — buses for congregation transport, vehicles for pastors, solar for their complexes. Approach church administrators and deacons. They often make fast decisions and pay reliably. Don't overlook this segment.
07
Leverage Returning Diaspora
Ghanaians returning from abroad (UK, US, Europe) often need a vehicle immediately and have funds to spend. They want something reliable and well-priced. Reach them through diaspora Facebook groups, WhatsApp groups, and friends/family networks. They trust recommendations from home.
08
Use Video Testimonials & Social Proof
Ask satisfied customers to record a 30-second WhatsApp video about their purchase. Ghanaians are heavily influenced by peer recommendations. Share these short clips in groups and on your status. "See what Mr Kwame said about his new SUV" is far more powerful than any ad you can write.
09
Handle Objections with the Comparison
"Is it not foreign used (Obroni Wawu)?" is the most common objection. Be ready: explain that these are brand new Chinese-manufactured vehicles with full documentation, manufacturer warranty, and competitive pricing vs Tokunbo. Show specs vs equivalent Japanese or European models at double the price.
10
Follow Up — Most Sales Happen on the 3rd–5th Contact
A prospect who didn't buy today is not a lost sale. In Ghana, decisions often involve family discussions, finances, and prayer. Follow up politely every 3–5 days with new inventory, a special offer, or just a "Good morning, just checking in." Persistence — without being pushy — wins.
11
Target Taxi & Ride-Hailing Operators
Bolt and Uber drivers and fleet operators are always looking for affordable, fuel-efficient vehicles. A saloon car or SUV on finance that earns income from day one is an easy sell. Target drivers who are currently renting vehicles — show them how owning through a loan is cheaper than renting long-term.
12
Your Referral Link Works 24/7 — Use It Everywhere
Put your link in your WhatsApp bio, Instagram bio, Facebook about section, and email signature. Every click through your link is tracked — even if they buy 3 months later. Passive sharing while you sleep is one of the easiest ways to earn commission without active selling.
